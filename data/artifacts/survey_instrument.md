# Survey instrument — wishlist non-conversion
_Generated 2026-08-20. Screener + 12 items, ~4 minutes._

## Why a survey as well as interviews

Interviews establish mechanism; they cannot establish prevalence from six people. The corpus gives prevalence of *discussion*, which over-counts loud barriers and under-counts silent ones — Stage A most of all, since forgetting produces no complaint. **A survey is the only instrument here that can measure a silent barrier**, and that is its job. Item S2 exists for exactly that reason.

## Screener

**S0.** In the last two months, have you saved an item to a wishlist or shopping list and not bought it? → *No: terminate.*

**S1.** When you saved it, which was closest to true? *(single choice — this is the segment assignment, and it must come before anything that could prime it)*
  a) I intended to buy it, soon
  b) I intended to buy it, but at some later point or occasion
  c) I was saving it to look at again, without a plan to buy
  d) I don't remember

**S2.** Since saving it, how many times have you opened your saved list? *(0 / 1–2 / 3–5 / more than 5 / I can't find my saved list)*
  > The last option is not a throwaway. Stage A is under-detected in the corpus by construction; this item is the one place it can be measured directly rather than inferred.

## Main items

**Q1.** Thinking of that item — what stopped you? *(select up to 2)*

  - Will the fabric, colour and quality match the photos?  _[C2 · 241 records in the corpus]_
  - Which size, when every brand runs different?  _[C1 · 189 records in the corpus]_
  - Will it look good on *me*, with what I own?  _[C3 · 154 records in the corpus]_
  - What did people who actually received it say?  _[C4 · 82 records in the corpus]_
  - I need to check with my partner/parent  _[C4.5 · 68 records in the corpus]_
  - Let me go check this properly somewhere else  _[C4.4 · 38 records in the corpus]_
  - If it's wrong, how painful is sending it back?  _[C7 · 140 records in the corpus]_
  - Is this the right price, and is now the right time?  _[C6 · 207 records in the corpus]_
  - What makes today different from tomorrow?  _[S14 · 51 records in the corpus]_
  - Something else — please say what
  - Nothing stopped me; I just haven't got round to it

  > The last two options carry weight. A forced-choice list of the corpus's own codes will reproduce the corpus's own ranking, which measures the instrument rather than the population. The residual share here is the check on that.

**Q2.** How close were you to buying it? *(1 = just looking, 5 = about to pay)*

**Q3.** Did you do anything to try to resolve it — search elsewhere, ask someone, check reviews, measure a garment you own? *(yes / no)*  
  **Q3a.** If yes: what did you do? *(free text)*
  > Workarounds are the strongest evidence in the corpus, because effort proves an unmet need without the person being asked. Measuring it here lets the corpus's workaround rates be checked against a population that did not self-select into posting.

**Q4.** Did you end up buying it, or something like it, anywhere? *(bought it here / bought elsewhere / bought something similar / no)*

**Q5.** What would have had to be true for you to buy it that day? *(free text)*

## Items testing specific hypotheses

**HYP-02** — Because size information does not reliably map to users’ bodies or brand variability, Indian online fashion shoppers who save apparel hesitate to choose a size and defer purchase, which is why sizing questions and measurement requests dominate a distinct sizing cluster and C1 discussions in the corpus.
  - Ask: an agree/disagree item stating the MECHANISM, not the outcome. Disagreement is the informative answer.
  - Kill condition: In 6 interviews with people who saved clothing but did not buy, if fewer than 2 can name a specific size/fit uncertainty they could not resolve (e.g., exact waist/hip fit, stretch, length on their height), the mechanism is wrong—the hesitation was not caused by size mapping.

**HYP-01** — Because product photos/descriptions underspecify tactile quality and durability, Indian online fashion shoppers who save items to wishlists leave to verify quality and then defer or drop the purchase, which is why strong discussion of the physical–digital quality gap appears alongside real‑buyer‑evidence and off‑platform‑verification patterns in the corpus.
  - Ask: an agree/disagree item stating the MECHANISM, not the outcome. Disagreement is the informative answer.
  - Kill condition: In interviews with 6 recent wishlist non‑buyers who mention quality/material doubt, if 4 or more report they did not seek any off‑platform validation before deciding not to buy (and still abandoned), the mechanism is wrong: the quality gap is not what drives the loss after saving.

**HYP-07** — Because platform‑native reviews/photos are seen as insufficient or untrustworthy, Indian online fashion wishlist savers go off‑platform to verify—and many do not return—which is why off‑platform verification workarounds (C14) appear alongside insufficient real‑buyer evidence (C4) and quality‑gap (C2) discussions in the corpus.
  - Ask: an agree/disagree item stating the MECHANISM, not the outcome. Disagreement is the informative answer.
  - Kill condition: In 6 interviews with shoppers who report leaving the app to check YouTube/Reddit before buying, if 5 or more say they usually come back and purchase the same saved item afterward, the mechanism is likely wrong—the off‑platform step is not causing the loss.

**HYP-03** — Because value signals and price timing are opaque, Indian online fashion shoppers who save items wait for clearer price anchors or the “right moment” and do not transact now, which is why value/price uncertainty discussions appear and are accompanied by ‘no trigger to act now’ counterfactuals in the corpus.
  - Ask: an agree/disagree item stating the MECHANISM, not the outcome. Disagreement is the informative answer.
  - Kill condition: In a short survey of 30 wishlist non‑buyers, if fewer than 10 select any reason indicating they were waiting for price clarity, a known sale/window, or fee transparency before deciding, this hypothesis fails—the delay is not driven by value/timing opacity.

**HYP-04** — Because shoppers distrust fulfilment and refund reliability, Indian online fashion wishlist savers avoid committing to purchases they may struggle to return, which is why returns‑risk complaints and workaround behaviors surface prominently in the corpus for C7.
  - Ask: an agree/disagree item stating the MECHANISM, not the outcome. Disagreement is the informative answer.
  - Kill condition: In 6 interviews with wishlist non‑buyers who mention platform trust, if at least 4 say they neither checked nor cared about the item’s return/refund policy or prior return experiences before deciding, the mechanism is wrong—distrust did not drive the avoidance.

## Analysis plan, fixed before fielding

- Report every result by the S1 segment. A pooled result mixes people with no purchase intent into a conversion question.
- **Pre-register the comparison:** the corpus's ranked barriers are listed in Q1 in corpus order. If the survey's order differs materially, the corpus's ranking is a ranking of *discussion*, not of incidence — which is what it has always claimed to be, and this is how that claim gets tested.
- Minimum n for a reported cell: 30, the same floor the corpus analysis uses.
