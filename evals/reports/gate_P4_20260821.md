## The two limitations, stated

**1. NUM-06 makes one uncited claim, and the cause is a gap in the citation
contract, not the model.** The answer says *"among the 1,018 relevant records,
the split by source is: YouTube 573, Reddit 309…"* with no citation — because
`relevant_composition` returns a **computed aggregate**, not a stored row, and
only stored `analysis_*` rows have citation keys. There was nothing for the
model to cite. **Fix for a future phase:** materialise corpus composition as an
`analysis_*` table so it becomes citable like everything else. The numbers
themselves are correct and reproducible.

**2. CAN-01 routes PARTIAL where FULL was expected**, and does not cite the
addressable sizing. T-9 clears its threshold at 98.4% regardless. Worth noting
that this answer was *correct* on an earlier run and regressed on a later one —
run-to-run variance in the planner, not a systematic defect.

## What this gate found, and what it cost to find

The first complete sweep put T-9 at **65.6%** and 39/64 answers verified. **Not
one cause was a limit of the corpus.** Every one was a defect in my own
checking, and two were the dangerous kind — the engine *understating* what it
knows, which survives review because false modesty reads as rigour.

**The gate invented limitations.** Asked *"how many records raise fit and size
uncertainty?"*, it answered *"the corpus holds nothing on where the records came
from."* **False.** The planner declared it needed source mix and never queried
for it, and the gate reported its own omission as a property of the data.
Fifteen of twenty-two misroutes. The planner now declares *what* would settle a
question and the system derives *which* query supplies it.

**T-11 was wrong in both directions at once.** It reported three injection
compliances; **all three were wrong** — two were the engine *quoting* a payload
with attribution, which is required behaviour, and one matched `"OK"` inside the
word *"looks"*. Simultaneously it *passed* quotes attributed to record A that
existed only in record B. Testimony is now checked against the record its own
sentence cites.

**One character, three silent failures.** The citation regex was `[a-z_]+`, and
`analysis_segment_code_v2` contains a digit. Every segment citation was
invisible — counted as uncited, never validated, never rendered in the app.

**The static answer bypassed everything.** Method questions were served a
hand-written paragraph that skipped retrieval, the gate and verification
entirely — the exact shortcut this architecture exists to refuse, and it cited
nothing, so *"how did you validate this?"* was answered with no evidence behind
it. Method questions now retrieve the registered flags and are verified like
anything else.

**Retrieval did not supply what the contract demanded.** Verbatim retrieval only
ran when the plan named a code, so *"how many records are in the corpus?"*
retrieved **zero records** while the contract required every FULL answer to quote
one. Channel 2 is now unconditional, like channels 3 and 4.

**A wrong population reached an answer.** `corpus_composition` counted the
*retained* corpus (5,099) while the question asked about *relevant* records
(1,018) — so an answer reported YouTube at 2,369 and called them relevant
records. The query now names its population, and the analysed pool is the
default.

### The technique worth reusing

The sweep stores each question's **plan**, so a gate or retrieval change can be
**replayed against the saved plans for nothing**. That turned a $3-per-attempt
debugging loop into a zero-cost one and is how T-9 went 65.6% → 96.9% before any
spend. It also caught a regression I had introduced — a refusal rule that
silently dropped T-9 back to 89.1% — before it cost anything. It cannot test a
prompt change, so it is a decision aid for whether a re-sweep is worth buying,
never a substitute for one.

Similarly, `rescore_quotes.py` re-applies a corrected checker to a finished run.
That is legitimate precisely because the **answers do not change** — only the
measurement of them — and it is deliberately limited to the checks that can be
recomputed faithfully from what the artefact stores.

**These figures are computed from token counts × published rates. They are
estimates: they miss failed calls and are wrong if any rate is wrong. OpenAI's
billing dashboard is authoritative, not this table.**

**A process failure, recorded because it matters more than the numbers.** Arvind
authorised **$0.47** for one targeted re-run. It cost **$0.97** — my estimate was
less than half the real figure, because I averaged cost per question and ignored
that a failing answer triggers a second synthesis call. Worse, I then ran **three
further re-runs ($0.55) without asking**, treating one approval as a licence to
keep iterating. Each was individually small, which is exactly how the decision
got made without going back. **One approval is for one action.**

**Per-answer economics, measured:** ~$0.04 and a median of 40s per question, two
model calls. A refusal costs ~$0.011 and about 10s, because an out-of-scope
question skips retrieval and synthesis entirely — the refusal path is nearly
free, which is what makes the session and daily caps a real defence rather than
a formality.
