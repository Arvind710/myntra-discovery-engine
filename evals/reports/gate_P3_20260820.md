## AC-6 — the novelty result, and how it was nearly wrong

**5 of 14 insights are confirmed outside H1–H15 / DH1–DH13.** The two that
matter:

**INS-02 · Fit uncertainty and approval-seeking are one event, not two.**
C1 and C10 co-occur at lift 2.93 (n_joint 37). The blueprint carries fit
uncertainty (DH5/H6/H11) and approval-and-permission (DH10) as separate
entries on the same list; nothing in the prior list says a shopper who cannot
resolve a size question outsources the decision to another person, and that the
wait for a reply *is* the deferral. The product consequence inverts what an
"approval barrier" implies: the fix is not a share-and-approve feature, it is
removing the need to ask.

**INS-08 · The blind clustering corroborates it from a direction that never
touched the C10 label.** Cluster `all|2` was formed by embedding similarity
with no codebook present and named by a model that is blocked at run time from
seeing any code name or definition (S2-CLU-4). It named the cluster *"Sizing
and measurements questions"* — and that one conversation space holds **113 C1
records and 37 C10 records**. Users describe asking-someone as part of the
sizing question. The split into two barriers with two different solves is the
codebook's, not theirs.

This corroboration is load-bearing, because **C10 is the least reliable code in
the corpus** (κ 0.10 against the human coder, who read it as *app* permissions
where the codebook means *another person's* approval). The claim survives only
because the cluster evidence is independent of that label. Any statement of
INS-02 must carry the caveat, and the app does.

The other three: **INS-12** (returns-trust is anti-correlated with price doubt
at lift 0.74 but travels with material doubt at 1.96 — a negative relational
finding, which a pre-registered list cannot contain by construction),
**INS-01** (C2×C4 at lift 2.73 plus C2.4 being 60.6% of C2 — the physical-digital
gap is largely a demand for proof, not a complaint about photography), and
**INS-10** (B1.2 and A1.2 at n=0, reported as *under-evidenced*, never as
absent).

### The filter was broken first, and reported a flattering result

The first novelty run flagged **14 of 14** insights as novel at a hand-picked
threshold of 0.62. That is not a discovery; it is a scale error. Two symptoms
made it obvious: the maximum similarity across all fourteen was 0.476, and
INS-13 — which merely restates the barrier ranking — scored the *lowest* of the
set, with its nearest prior on an unrelated code.

**Reporting 14/14 would have satisfied AC-6 by broken measurement**, which is
EC-INS-7's failure mode wearing the opposite mask. Two fixes:

1. **The priors were too thin to embed against.** Each was four or five words
   of code name. They now carry the code's name, its unresolved question, its
   boundary note and its observable workarounds — comparable in substance to a
   generated insight, so the score measures meaning rather than sentence length.
2. **The threshold is now measured, not chosen.** A control set of 15
   deliberate restatements — non-novel by construction — is embedded against
   the same priors. Their similarity to the nearest prior runs min 0.402,
   median 0.681, max 0.750. The flag line is the 5th percentile: **0.519**.
   That converts "is 0.62 right?" into a number anyone can re-run.

The recalibrated filter shortlists 10 of 14. **The verdicts are then made by
hand** and committed to `codebook/novelty_verdicts.yaml`, because similarity
separates a *methodological* claim from a *barrier hypothesis* by form
regardless of content — four of the ten flagged are recorded there as **not**
novel for exactly that reason. `novelty.py --verdicts` refuses to apply the
file if the insights have been regenerated underneath it, and a test asserts
the same.

## The second instrument correction: sub-code shares were double-counted

The sub-code roll-up added to `crosstabs.py` first reported **C2.4 at 120% of
C2**. `subcodes` keys on `run_id`, C2 and C3 were each sub-coded twice (a re-run
after a prompt fix), and a naive `GROUP BY` counted the same record under both
runs. The two runs also covered *different populations* — C3 had 151 records in
one and 188 in the other — so mixing them is not a smaller version of the same
error, it is two analyses added together.

Fixed by taking the latest run per theme and honouring the corpus exclusions.
**This moves two figures that were previously quoted:**

| | previously quoted | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| C2.4 as a share of C2 | 71.5% | **60.6%** |
| C3.1 as a share of C3 | 84.6% | **77.3%** |

Both claims survive — C2.4 and C3.1 remain dominant — but the old numbers must
not be repeated. The Analysis page's sub-code tab had the same fault and now
reads the materialised roll-up instead of aggregating the raw table.

## What is generated, and what is deliberately not

**Insights: 14 kept, 0 rejected on the final pass.** An earlier pass rejected 2
of 14 — one for citing the wrong row for a true claim, one for a malformed
citation key — and both were recovered by a single bounded repair pass. The
repair is capped at one attempt, never loops, and the checker runs again
afterwards.

The malformed key is worth naming: the model wrote `4 | Stuck Deciders`,
reading the first data column of the packet as part of the citation key. The
fix was to render the key in backticks with a `::` separator, **not** to loosen
the checker — that would have discarded the contract to work around a display
bug.

**Hypotheses: 8 kept, 0 rejected.** The model writes the causal claim, the
mechanism, the falsifier and the reading of the counter-evidence. It does *not*
write the supporting count, the verbatims, or the source diversity — those are
computed from the classifications, and a test re-derives all sixteen values and
requires exact agreement. Verbatims are drawn only from rows with
`span_verified = 1`, spread across distinct authors and sources.

Counter-evidence is *retrieved before it is asked for*: `counter_signals()`
computes the anti-correlations, single-source concentrations, low classifier
confidences and known κ failures per code and puts them in the prompt. A model
asked "what contradicts this?" with nothing in front of it writes a hedge.
Given the actual objections it has to engage — and it did, including naming
C10's κ 0.10 in the hypothesis that rests on C10.

**Artefacts** (interview guide, survey instrument, problem-framing canvas) are
deterministic templates over the hypotheses — no model call, so they cannot
introduce a claim the corpus does not carry. Ordered by confidence, so the
low-confidence C10 hypothesis does not lead the research plan. A test asserts
none of them proposes a discount, coupon or cashback: C-2 binds the output, not
only the analysis.

## Stated limitations carried into P4 and the deck

1. **C10 (framework C4.5) is unreliable — κ 0.10.** It is the fifth-ranked
   opportunity and half of the headline novel insight. The cluster evidence is
   independent of the label, which is the only reason the claim stands.
2. **`segment_fit` is partly circular.** Segments are derived from the
   classification, and "not decided" is the presence of a Confidence-phase
   code — so Confidence codes are barred from three of the six segments by
   definition. The ranking does not depend on it (the leave-one-out check
   confirms), and the app says so with a slider that goes to zero.
3. **C6 is over-represented in Play Store reviews** — 52.6% share there against
   20.3% corpus-wide, JS divergence 0.398. Any C6 claim needs that qualifier.
4. **T-4 and T-2 still fail** and are still reported as failing, now on Home as
   well as the Validation tab.
5. **The priors are a reconstruction**, not a transcription. The blueprint's
   H/DH prose lives in a PDF outside the repository; each prior is the union of
   the claims made by the codes that absorbed it. This makes novelty *harder*
   to claim, never easier — an insight must miss the whole framework rather
   than one sentence — which is the failure direction that keeps the test
   honest.
6. **`analysis_addressable` excludes Collectors from the opportunity but not
   from the corpus.** Every share on the Analysis page still uses the 1,018
   denominator; every share on the Insights page uses 892. Both are labelled,
   but they are different denominators on adjacent pages and a reader could
   conflate them.

## S3-HUM-1 — my own answer, pending Arvind's

*Would I defend this opportunity choice to a sceptical mentor using only this
evidence?*

**Yes for the ranking, with one reservation about the headline.**

The ranking is the strong part. C2 leads on 241 records from 226 distinct
authors across all four sources, holds first place in 99.6% of perturbed
weightings, survives five of six leave-one-out checks, and does not depend on
the one component I know to be partly circular. The stage-level conclusion
survives a 6.6× correction for the bias I can name. I would put that in front
of a sceptic without hedging.

The reservation is INS-02, the novel finding, which is also the most
interesting thing here. It rests on a code with κ 0.10. The blind-cluster
corroboration is genuinely independent and I think it carries the claim — but
"carries the claim" means *worth five interviews*, not *worth building on*.
HYP-08 exists precisely to kill it, and its falsifier is the sharpest of the
eight. If a mentor pushed, I would say: the ranking is a finding, and the novel
insight is a lead.

What I would not defend is any statement of these numbers as funnel
measurements. They are shares of discussion. Every page says so, and that
restraint is the reason the rest is defensible.
